Orbit compute is funded by defence and launch money, not by cloud customers

Four lines of inquiry and 41 web pages point the same way: the first contracts buy processing for data that is already in space, and public programmes carry most of the risk.

14 web sources citedFinished in 6 min 12 sNot yet saved

The money arriving in orbital computing comes from three places, and none of them is the enterprise cloud buyer the pitch decks describe. The largest share is defence and Earth-observation budgets that pay to process imagery before it is sent down.1 Launch providers come second, bundling compute payloads to fill capacity they have already sold.2

Terms favour the buyer

Contracts published so far are milestone-based: payment follows demonstrated uptime in orbit, not delivery of hardware.3 Insurers price the radiation risk to chips high enough that most operators self-insure the first units.4 Public programmes in the EU and the US underwrite the rest through research grants rather than equity.
