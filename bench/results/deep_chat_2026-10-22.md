`scripts/bench_deep_chat.py` (gold: `bench/fixture_deep_chat_KI.json`).

- **A (baseline):** commit `3376140` — before the answer-changing tweaks (it already has
  tweak 1 and the safe batch: pre-flight, auto-read, quote check, hit hygiene, tool errors).
- **B (current):** commit `a4353bc` — adds the word floor per source, the evidence ledger with
  synthesis and the stop rule, Gemma sampling (temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 64) with

## Setup (not the GPU box)

### Findings

1. **The reworked stop rule works as intended:** both failing ledger answers went back to

- When a ledger answer cites an unread §, drop that claim or tell the ledger prompt to

baseline `3376140`, each on indexes built by its own code. Gold: StrlSchV § 31
(Dosiskriterium). Rows: `deep_chat_2026-10-24_q6_f2634db.json`,
`deep_chat_2026-10-24_q6_base_3376140.json`. (The scratchpad was wiped during the current

current format for the current run.)

| Run | Exit | Gold read | Overlap § 31 | Unread | Calls | s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline, 2026-10-22 | submit-accepted | 1/1 | 0.86 | 0 | – | 524 |
| B-off `4071c7a`, 2026-10-23 | (unlabelled) | 1/1 | 0.25 | 0 | – | 343 |
| **Baseline, this run** | submit-accepted | 1/1 | 0.25 | 0 | 14 | 518 |
| **Current `f2634db`** | best-effort | 1/1 | 0.29 | 0 | 10 | 602 |

Gate events (current): only "ledger answer rejected: 1 distinct searches so far, minimum is
3" (at the final step; the stop rule never fired, the model's own answer failed).

### Findings

1. **The ledger answer was right but blocked by the search minimum.** It opens with the
   gold fact (10 µSv per calendar year, StrlSchV § 31 (2)), cites § 31, § 37, § 200, § 99,
   and was rejected only because the run made one distinct search: the pre-flight. The
   model never searched itself; it read six sections instead (§ 31, § 37, § 49, § 58,
   § 99, § 200). Counting the pre-flight as one search did not cause this (as two it would
   still be short of three), but it shows that the minimum blocks a code-written answer
   whose grounding is the reads.
2. **A bug in the gate's section lookup (verified).** `tools._resolve_section` falls back to
   a prefix match without a number boundary, so a tag `StrlSchV.md § 31 (2)` resolves to
   **§ 3**. Gemma writes paragraph numbers into tags ("§ 31 (2)", "§ 99 (1)", "§ 58 (3)"),
   so a correctly cited, read section is judged as an unread § 3 or § 9. This is the likely
   cause of q5's "you cite § 2 … § 3 … never read" (tags like "§ 29 (1)" or "§ 30 (2)").
   It affects the agent's own answers too, not only the ledger answer.
3. **The prompt line is not obeyed.** Despite "name no § outside the tags", the ledger
   answer names § 4, § 5, § 37 and § 42 in prose and tags a whole-file quote as
   `[Source: StrlSchV.md § 4]` (the quote came from `StrlSchV.md` without a §). So q5's
   second cause is real too: E4B writes a § into a tag the quote did not carry.
4. **The ledger is noisy.** Of 25 quotes, 9 are off-topic (§ 49 Fachkunde, § 58
   Kontamination, StrlSchV definitions), matched on generic stems ("radioaktiv", "Stoffe").
   The ledger answer copies them into a "Kontamination und Überwachung" section.
5. **Time and outcome:** current 602 s (1.16x) and best-effort, baseline 518 s and accepted.
   Both read the gold §, with overlap about even (0.29 against 0.25).

### Recommendation

- **Fix the prefix lookup** (bug, fix first): a prefix match must end at a number boundary
  (`§ 3` must not match `§ 31 (2)`), and a trailing paragraph "(2)" or "Abs. 2" should
  resolve to its §. Red test: `§ 31 (2)` resolves to § 31.
- **Normalise the ledger answer's tags in code** before the gate: map each tag to the
  ledger ref with the same file and §, and drop a claim whose tag has no ledger ref. This
  replaces the prompt line, which E4B ignores.
- **Decide the search minimum for the ledger answer** (needs your call): either try the
  ledger answer only on a covered run (≥ 3 searches, as the stop rule already does), saving
  the generation, or count the run's distinct read sections towards the minimum.
- Later: raise the ledger's match threshold (or weigh rarer stems), so generic legal words
  do not fill it.
